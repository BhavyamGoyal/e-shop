Power path not in firmware

FromToNotes
USB-C port (right foot)TP4056 board IN+ / IN−Port is on the TP4056 PCB (USB-C version), window cut in the right foot. If your board has only a micro-USB port, use that instead.
TP4056 B+ / B−Battery + / −IS-NO-IS 16046, 3.6 V 900 mAh, sits in the head, right side. Charge current is 1 A by default, which is above 1C for a 900 mAh cell: change the RPROG resistor to 2 kΩ (about 580 mA) or 3 kΩ (about 400 mA).
TP4056 OUT+ / OUT−5 V boost module IN+ / IN−Only on TP4056 boards with the DW01 protection chip (6 pads: IN, OUT, B). OUT is 3.0 to 4.2 V, not 5 V.
Boost OUT+ESP32 VIN / 5VFeeds the on-board regulator. Set the boost to 5.0 V before connecting the ESP32.
Boost OUT−ESP32 GNDCommon ground for every module
The firmware has no battery or charger code, so this path is the usual arrangement and is not confirmed by the repo. Check it against your actual boards before soldering.
Check the battery label: the TP4056 charges to 4.2 V and only suits a rechargeable Li-ion cell. A 3.6 V cell can also be a non-rechargeable lithium thionyl chloride cell, and charging that one is dangerous.
Feeding the battery straight into ESP32 VIN is not reliable: the on-board regulator needs about 4.5 V in to hold 3.3 V, and a 3.6 to 4.2 V cell falls short, which causes brown-outs. Use a small boost (MT3608 or similar) set to 5 V. Use the TP4056 OUT pads, not B, so the protection chip guards against over-discharge. The ESP32 draws about 80 to 240 mA with Wi-Fi, so expect roughly 3 to 5 hours of runtime from 900 mAh through the boost.

Power wiring, working version use this

positive negative / ground
StepDo this
1Solder the battery to the TP4056 B+ / B−. Check polarity first.
2Solder TP4056 OUT+ to one side of the slide switch, and the other side of the switch to boost IN+. Solder TP4056 OUT− straight to boost IN−.
3With the switch on and nothing on the boost output, turn the boost trimmer until a multimeter reads 5.0 V across boost OUT+ / OUT−.
4Solder boost OUT+ to ESP32 VIN and boost OUT− to ESP32 GND. Add the capacitor across the boost output if you have one (long leg to OUT+).
5Plug USB-C into the TP4056 to charge. The bot runs from the battery with the switch on, and can charge while running.
Join the ESP32 GND to the shared module ground (the bus from the first diagram), so the screen, touch and vibration modules all sit on the same ground as the boost.
Do not connect USB to the ESP32 at the same time as the boost output is on VIN without a diode, or the two 5 V sources can back-feed each other. Use the TP4056 port for charging and power, and unplug the boost output from VIN while flashing over the ESP32's own USB port.

The model draws four wires per module. The neck carries 16 wires and each foot passage carries 4. The ESP32 end takes 24 wires, but only 3V3 and GND need sharing: join them with a splice or a small bus so the ESP32's few 3V3 and GND pins are not overloaded.

Firmware notes

  • pins.config.h is the source of truth. docs/hardware.md is out of date: it lists OLED SDA as GPIO21 and touch as GPIO17, but the code uses GPIO23 and GPIO21.
  • The model's note "4 wires per module" holds for the screen and accelerometer. Touch and vibration use only 3.
  • Vibration is defined in pins.config.h but not driven anywhere yet: the reminder action falls back to text with "unsupported". A bare coin motor must not be driven straight from a GPIO, so confirm the V919 board has its own driver transistor.
  • GPIO21 and GPIO27 are not strapping pins, so the touch modules are safe at boot.
